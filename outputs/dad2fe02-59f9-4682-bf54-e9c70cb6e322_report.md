## Executive Summary
Adopt a **Managed AI Service from a Tier‑1 Cloud Provider** as the primary AI‑agent platform. This approach delivers the highest combined score for compliance (7), security (7), scalability (9) and total cost of ownership (8), while keeping overall risk at a manageable level (8) and earning a total rating of **7.8** (Decision Matrix). Expected outcomes include accelerated time‑to‑value for AI‑driven banking services, robust regulatory alignment, and a secure, scalable infrastructure that can be expanded across business lines. The biggest risk is **incomplete regulatory mapping from data gaps**, classified as Critical (Risk Register), which could expose the bank to non‑compliance penalties if AI use cases are not fully linked to evolving regulations.

## Key Findings
1. **Regulatory Landscape is Fragmented and Evolving** – Banks must map AI use cases to a growing set of rules such as the EU AI Act (Art 5, 9) and US OCC Bulletin 2023‑29, otherwise they face critical compliance gaps【1†source】,【2†source】.  
2. **Managed Cloud Services Offer Superior Scale and Security** – Tier‑1 cloud providers score highest on scalability (9) and security (7) in the decision matrix, indicating they can meet the high‑volume transaction demands of banking while maintaining strong protection controls【4†source】.  
3. **Model Bias and Explainability Remain High‑Priority Risks** – Limited training data can introduce bias, and lack of explainability hampers auditability, both flagged as High‑risk items that require bias‑testing frameworks and XAI layers【5†source】,【11†source】.  
4. **Third‑Party Vendor Risk Must Be Managed Rigorously** – Cybersecurity exposure from external AI platforms is Critical; a formal third‑party risk assessment with data‑flow mapping is essential【10†source】.  
5. **Hybrid or In‑House Solutions Lag on Cost‑Effectiveness** – In‑house platforms and hybrid edge deployments score lower on TCO and overall rating, making them less attractive for rapid AI adoption【4†source】.

## Recommendations
1. **Adopt Managed AI Service from Tier‑1 Cloud Provider**  
   - *Rationale*: Highest overall rating (7.8) with strong compliance, security, and scalability scores【4†source】.  
   - *Owner*: Chief Technology Officer (CTO)  
   - *90‑day first step*: Execute a vendor selection RFP, include clauses for quarterly regulatory inventory updates (per Risk 1 mitigation) and third‑party security assessments (per Risk 5).  

2. **Implement a Bias‑Testing & Explainability Framework**  
   - *Rationale*: Addresses High‑risk model bias and lack of auditability (Risks 2 & 7)【5†source】,【11†source】.  
   - *Owner*: Head of AI/ML (bias) & Chief Compliance Officer (explainability)  
   - *90‑day first step*: Deploy synthetic data generators and integrate an XAI layer into the pilot AI agent; schedule third‑party audit for the first release.  

3. **Establish an AI Governance Board**  
   - *Rationale*: Mitigates strategic misalignment (Risk 4) and ensures cross‑functional KPI tracking【5†source】.  
   - *Owner*: Chief Strategy Officer (CSO)  
   - *90‑day first step*: Form the board with representation from CRO, CISO, CTO, and Business Units; define quarterly review calendar and KPI dashboard.  

4. **Deploy a Sandbox with Synthetic Transaction Data**  
   - *Rationale*: Validates model performance despite architectural data gaps (Risk 3) and supports drift detection (Risk 6)【5†source】.  
   - *Owner*: CTO  
   - *90‑day first step*: Set up a secure sandbox environment on the chosen cloud platform; populate with synthetic data and run end‑to‑end test cases for the first AI agent use case.  

5. **Negotiate Robust Third‑Party Risk Clauses**  
   - *Rationale*: Reduces Critical cybersecurity exposure (Risk 5) and vendor lock‑in (Risk 8)【10†source】.  
   - *Owner*: CISO  
   - *90‑day first step*: Draft and circulate a standard third‑party risk assessment questionnaire; require data‑flow maps and exit provisions in all AI vendor contracts.
